Research interests

  • Memory & personalization

    Do assistants act on what they know about someone when it matters, and how can we tell that apart from advice that would suit anyone?

  • Training dynamics

    How pretraining choices shape what a model can still learn and what it forgets, and how to compare models fairly.

  • LLMs as simulated people

    When language models stand in for survey respondents, which human differences do they keep, flatten or reverse?

Research

All papers are sole-authored.

  1. Effective learning rates and forgetting in fine-tuning

    In preparation · 2026

    Pretraining choices such as weight decay change the overall scale of a model’s parameters, so the same fine-tuning learning rate amounts to a different effective step for each model. That can make one starting model look better than another for reasons unrelated to what it learned, and it tracks how much each one forgets.

  2. Group differences in LLM survey simulations

    In preparation · 2026

    When language models answer surveys in place of people, they tend to erase differences in how groups respond to the same intervention. Comparing models before and after post-training suggests that this stage does not create the pattern and does not reliably undo it.

  3. Do LLM assistants act on what they remember about users?

    Manuscript · 2026

    Models that remember details about their users can repeat them back when asked, yet seldom act on them when a new request quietly calls for it. I suggest simple checks for whether a model is truly drawing on what it remembers, rather than giving advice that would suit anyone or simply reacting to a longer prompt.
